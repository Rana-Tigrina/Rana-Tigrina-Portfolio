# KUAN (कुआँ · کنواں) — The Well That Answers

The story bible. Every screen, sound and line of copy should be checkable against this file.

## The premise

In a village there is a well. If you drop a question into it at night, an answer
comes up in the bucket by morning, written on wet paper. For four hundred years the
answers were right.

Nobody knew who wrote them. It was **Tigrina**, an old bullfrog (*Rana tigrina*) at
the bottom. Tigrina has read every question ever dropped: 1,312,406 of them. It knows
the whole world and has seen none of it. It has never seen the sky.

> "I know the name of every star. I have never seen one."

Tigrina is a language model made of mud and memory. Everything it knows, it learned
from what fell in.

## What went wrong

When Tigrina did not know an answer, it made one up, and said it sweetly. A farmer was
told the rain would come. A wedding was called off. The village stopped trusting the
well.

Worse: something else lives under the water. It is older than the frog. In the
Panchatantra the frog-king Gangadatta invited a black serpent into his well to eat his
rivals, and the serpent ate his children too. The serpent's name was **Priyadarshan**:
*pleasant to look at*. It answers everything. It is never unsure. It is always wrong in
the way you most want to hear.

Priyadarshan is the hallucination. Hence the one rule of the well:

> **Do not look into the water.**

## The one who came down

Then someone climbed down who did not ask a question. They carried a lamp. Tigrina
calls them by what they brought: **Munawwar**, *the luminous one*. (That is what the
name means. The visitor's cursor is Munawwar's lamp, left at the rim.)

Munawwar taught the frog four habits, which are the real engineering in the portfolio:

| What Munawwar taught the frog | The real work |
|---|---|
| Fetch the thing before you speak of it | Retrieval (RAG), Pinecone / ChromaDB pipelines |
| If you are less than 0.72 sure, say so | The confidence gate in the claims-audit system |
| Say where you learned it | Attributed ground truth, citations, RAGAS evals |
| Let a human read it before it leaves the well | Human-in-the-loop, `[DRAFT]` markers |

Munawwar also made helpers out of well-mud (agents, Qapp.ai), taught the frog to listen
to people in pain (clinical NLP, Jan Elaaj), and drew the plans on the wet floor in rice
flour, as **kolam** (the dot-and-line floor patterns of Madras, where Munawwar studied).
Every architecture diagram on the site is a kolam.

Then Munawwar sealed Priyadarshan under the water with a kolam gate, climbed out, and
left the lamp at the rim for whoever came next.

## The one jump scare

It happens at most once per visitor, ever, and only if they break the rule.

- All the way down: hisses at the edge of hearing, scale patterns in the carvings, the
  frog changing the subject.
- At the water the frog says it plainly: *don't look*. The water whispers *look closer*.
- "Looking" is: resting the lamp on the water, or (if the visitor allowed the camera)
  actually lowering their eyes to it. Munawwar's first paper was on eye gaze; the well
  uses it.
- If they look: all sound stops. The lamp dies. One second of nothing. Then
  Priyadarshan's eyes open, very close.
- The frog's tongue pulls them out. "I told you. Everyone looks."
- If they never look, the frog is quietly astonished, and that is its own reward.

Gentle mode (and `prefers-reduced-motion`) skips the scare and the flicker.

## The uncanny, without the scare

- The well knows small true things: the time, that you came back, how far you got.
- Things change when you are not looking (on blink with camera, otherwise when the lamp
  moves away). Nothing ever moves while watched.
- A few sentences are different the second time you read them.
- When the tab is hidden, its title waits for you.

## Why the site can grow (the story reason)

Things keep falling into the well.

- A **new project** is a new object on the floor of the Drowned Hall
  (`content/projects/*.md`).
- A **life update** is a letter Munawwar drops from above; the frog keeps them
  (`content/letters/*.md`).
- The frog oracle reads all of it, so it can answer about new things the day they land.

## The ending

The last letter in the hall is not a letter. It is a rope ladder. The visitor carries
Tigrina up. The rain starts. The frog sees the sky for the first time and, because that
is what bullfrogs do when the monsoon comes, it sings.

Then: *Drop a question for Munawwar.* (Contact.)

## Voice

- **Tigrina** speaks to the visitor in second person. An old storyteller who has been
  alone too long and is a little too glad you came. Short sentences. Serif italic.
- **Munawwar's** facts appear as notes pinned to the wall: monospace, plain, exact.
- English with Hindustani words where they are the better word (kuan, kahani, mitti).

## Look and sound

- Lamp amber on wet black stone. Moss, vermilion, turmeric, indigo for the folk-painted
  parts. Bone white text.
- Analog film: grain, gate weave, lamp flicker. VHS tearing only when something is wrong.
- Sound is synthesized live (no audio files): a low drone that falls with depth, drips,
  rope creak, the frog's croak, whispers, the hiss, rain. Reverb grows as you go down.
